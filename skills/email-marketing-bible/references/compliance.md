# Compliance

Read this when: any question about consent, region, unsubscribe, address, penalties, AI disclosure or truthful offers.
Last checked: 9 Oct 2026

A working summary for agents, not legal advice. When an answer turns on a figure, a deadline or a borderline case, verify it at the primary source or ask for counsel. The core §4 checklist runs these six questions before every send; any unclear answer means refuse or ask.

## 1. The six send-time questions

1. **Type: transactional, lifecycle, marketing, newsletter or cold?** The primary purpose of the content decides it, not an ESP setting (CAN-SPAM's primary-purpose test, [primary] https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business). Each type has its own consent basis and, by default, its own subdomain (core §0).
2. **Recipient region?** Rules follow the recipient, not the sender. A list with people in several regions needs the strictest applicable rule for each person, or region-split sends.
3. **Consent basis for this audience and this content, including buyers?** A purchase is not automatically marketing consent: confirm the permission (express consent, or a soft opt-in where the regime allows one) for this content. Keep the record: source, date, wording shown.
4. **One-click unsubscribe and physical address present?** Section 3 and the regime table.
5. **Suppressions applied?** Unsubscribed, bounced, complained and globally suppressed contacts, per channel. Australia's regulator treated marketing sent to customers who had opted out of one channel as sent without consent: TAB self-reported 217,000 such emails and SMS over 16 days, and paid a A$1,254,000 spam infringement notice (ACMA, 22 Jul 2026) [primary] https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-07/tab-pays-27m-telemarketing-and-spam-breaches ; https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/Tabcorp%20Holdings%20Limited%20-%20Infringement%20Notice%20-%20Spam%20Act%202003_Redacted.pdf
6. **Content materially accurate, with deadlines, scarcity and discounts real?** Section 4.

## 3. Unsubscribe mechanics

- One-click unsubscribe is required for bulk marketing by Gmail (RFC 8058) and Yahoo (which highly recommends the RFC 8058 method); Microsoft lists a functional unsubscribe link as a recommendation, not a requirement [primary] https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126 ; https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/ ; the Microsoft post in deliverability.md.
- Honour it fast: Google recommends bulk senders fulfil unsubscribe requests within 48 hours, and senders who don't lose access to Gmail's delivery support and mitigations [primary] https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414. Yahoo asks for the same within 2 days [primary] https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/. The legal limits above (10 business days in the US and Canada, 5 in Australia) are ceilings, not targets.
- It must work with no login and no extra step. Gmail's Manage subscriptions view and personal agents now unsubscribe in bulk, and a broken exit turns into a complaint.
- Unsubscribing never depends on deleting an account or logging in.
- Keep a visible unsubscribe link in the footer as well. Re-check the footer and unsubscribe after every template edit, because an agent's edit can drop them.
- Opt-outs are per channel and binding; consent never travels across channels (TAB, section 1; messaging-channels.md).

## 4. Truthful offers

Deadlines, scarcity and discounts must be real: the calendar honours the deadline, the stock limit exists, the discount is the price at checkout. In Washington a fake subject-line deadline is a per-message liability (regime table). Meta's connector rules for its Muse agent forbid fabricated urgency or discounts outright [primary] https://muse.ai/platform/docs. An agent that writes urgency copy reads the offer's real end date from the source of truth; it never invents one. If an offer is extended, stop sending "last chance" or "ends tonight" for it, and keep proof of every deadline and stock claim: the real end time, a stock snapshot, which segment saw it (r/Emailmarketing, Sep 2026) [directional] https://www.reddit.com/r/Emailmarketing/comments/1wj60fq/theres_a_wave_of_lawsuits_right_now_over_last/

## 5. AI disclosure and provenance

- **EU AI Act Article 50** (from 2 Aug 2026; Commission guidelines and FAQ above) [primary]:
  - No blanket "made with AI" label is needed for routine marketing email. Copy that makes health, consumer-safety or sustainability claims, or a newsletter on public-interest topics, may need genuine human editorial review or a label.
  - Label realistic synthetic people, places or scenes that could pass as real. For ads the Commission calls this case-specific: a realistic synthetic influencer or an AI product image that misleads about the product needs a label; an accurately shown real product on an AI-generated background generally doesn't.
  - An AI agent that writes to or converses with people (reply handling, support, outbound) must not pass as human and must say who it acts for. This is primarily a provider duty under Article 50(1), so treat it as a rule for any agent you run. The Commission's own example is an email generated by an AI agent with an AI label at the top.
  - Practitioner reading: Mike Nelson, "Disclosure Day" (Really Good Emails, 4 Sep 2026) [trade] https://reallygoodemails.com/school/blog/ai-disclaimers-in-email
- **New York** requires a conspicuous disclosure when an ad contains an AI-generated "synthetic performer", in force from 9 Jun 2026 [primary] https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8420/amendment/A
- **Watermarks.** Claude marks generated text, including text it only proofreads or translates [primary] https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773, and on 5 Oct 2026 OpenAI said it would watermark eligible ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU within weeks, with API watermarking opt-in [primary] https://openai.com/index/eu-text-provenance/. No mailbox provider is known to read these marks. Never promise "undetectable" AI copy.
- **Provenance log.** Keep your own record per send (model used, what the AI produced or edited, who reviewed it, what shipped). Embedded image provenance can be lost when files are resized or recompressed (Nelson, above) [trade].

## 6. Cold email legal line

Check jurisdiction, recipient type and basis before any cold send.

- Germany needs prior consent for B2B email (UWG §7) [primary] https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html
- Canada and Australia require consent; inferred consent there is narrow, so check it rather than assume it.
- A platform's acceptable-use policy is not a legal basis.
- CAN-SPAM allowing a cold email does not make the contact a subscriber. Cold contacts never move into a marketing list or newsletter without opting in.
- An agent that sends cold email is still sending commercial email: same rules, same unsubscribe and address (cold-email.md).

**AI does not transfer liability.** You own an agent's sends; never trust it to preserve the unsubscribe or footer when it edits a template.
