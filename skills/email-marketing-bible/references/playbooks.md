Last checked: 9 Oct 2026

Playbooks for 19 verticals: five in depth here, the rest summarised, each with a pointer to chapter 11 (`emailmarketingskill.com/11-industry-playbooks/`) for more. Figures carried over from the long form are labelled [directional]; check them against the account's own data. Seasonal plans and BFCM are in flows.md. ## In depth

**Ecommerce DTC.** Email drives a large share of revenue (25-40% is often quoted) [directional]. Build welcome, abandoned cart and post-purchase first; the profit sits in the flows nobody watches (post-purchase, replenishment, win-back). Send more to engaged tiers and less to lapsing ones (thresholds.md). Use RFM to separate VIPs (early access), at-risk (re-engagement) and lapsed (win-back or suppress). Year-in-review emails built from each customer's own purchase data are a strong seasonal send. Some orders now arrive through shopping agents with no email click: give them their own attribution channel (measurement.md) and keep the offer price identical everywhere an agent might compare it.

**SaaS B2B.** Activation, not opens. Trigger onboarding on what the user has and hasn't done (completed setup, invited the team, used the core feature), not on calendar days; this needs product events flowing to the ESP, which an agent connected over MCP can scaffold from a brief. One CTA per email. Keep educating after onboarding with feature-adoption emails rather than going quiet until renewal. Segment by company size and use case. If sign-in uses magic links, remember a user's agent may never see them (design.md).

**SaaS B2C.** Retention drives the economics. Re-engage early: at about 7 days inactive, then 14 and 21, each email showing what the user is missing. Usage-based emails ("you processed 47 invoices last month") beat calendar-based announcements [directional]. Gamification works only when the milestone means something.

**Newsletter and creator.** Monetisation inflects around 10K subscribers [directional]. Revenue stacks in order: sponsorships, then paid subscriptions, then affiliates, then products, then events. Referral programmes help growth [directional]. Consistency beats polish: a 20-minute weekly (Liz Wilcox) or 2-3 short sends a week (Ian Brodie) beat one polished monthly. Assume part of the audience reads you through an AI summary: give each issue one specific claim worth clicking for, and judge on clicks, replies and paid retention, not opens. Subscribers who open everything may be machines (Finn Tropy, Aug 2026) [directional] https://finntropy.substack.com/p/i-dont-read-your-newsletter-either. On Substack any reader can scan an issue for AI text, so keep the opinion and the story in the writer's own words and add a one-line process note if AI helped [primary] https://post.substack.com/p/against-claudefishing

**Nonprofit.** Turn one-time donors into recurring supporters. Roughly 3:1 value to ask: impact reports, beneficiary stories and behind-the-scenes updates between appeals. Make the supporter the hero and the ask specific ("your $50 provided..."). Separate cancelled donations (a relationship problem) from failed payments (a technical one) and answer each differently. Start the year-end campaign in November, not the last two weeks of December. UK charities can use a soft opt-in for supporters since 5 Feb 2026 (compliance.md) [primary].

## Summaries

- **Agency.** Benchmark each client against its own vertical, run a monthly testing calendar per client and share what each test teaches across the team, and hold quarterly strategy reviews so no programme runs on autopilot. Deep platform specialisation tends to beat being a generalist [practitioner].
- **Healthcare (regulated).** Keep marketing completely separate from clinical communications, on different domains and systems. Health privacy law (HIPAA in the US) and advertising rules apply.
- **Financial services (regulated).** Build compliance review into the production timeline, with pre-approved modules for disclaimers and risk warnings. Transactional emails are the best cross-sell slot, within the rules.
- **Real estate.** Speed to lead wins: reply within minutes. Segment drips by stage (browsing, actively searching, under contract, past clients) and reference the properties the prospect actually viewed.
- **Travel and hospitality.** Map email to the trip: inspiration, planning, booking (abandoned-booking recovery), pre-trip (upsells 3-5 days before arrival), in-stay, post-trip (reviews, loyalty, rebooking). Lead seasonal booking peaks by 4-8 weeks [directional].
- **Education.** Coordinate before you create: consolidate departmental sends into one digest and segment by stage (prospective, admitted, enrolled, alumni). Plain emails from a named counsellor can beat branded HTML for prospective students [directional].
- **Professional services.** Long sales cycles need frequent, short emails that mix useful ideas with a clear call to action (Ian Brodie: at least weekly). Send a timely analysis within days of a major regulatory change.
- **Retail.** Loyalty and personalised offers. Points-balance and expiry reminders before points lapse; birthday offers; VIP early access for the top decile by spend; review requests 10-14 days after delivery.
- **Events.** Start nurturing at registration (what to expect, schedule, logistics), follow up within a week of the event (thanks, recordings, survey), and personalise follow-up by sessions attended. Recurring events: the post-event sequence is the top of next year's funnel.
- **B2B manufacturing.** Reorder triggers are the highest-value automation: remind shortly before the customer's usual reorder interval, with current pricing and a one-click reorder link.
- **Restaurant and food.** Connect loyalty data so each email shows points and progress. Match frequency to the occasion (coffee weekly or more, fine dining a few times a month). Trigger "we haven't seen you" after about twice the customer's usual visit interval.
- **Fitness.** Everything in the welcome series aims at the first visit within 7 days. Celebrate milestones; seasonal challenges work well.
- **Media and publishing.** Relevance by topic beats demographics. Use the 1-3-1 structure (one story, three items, one CTA), keep the first ad placement near the top, and label sponsored content clearly. Build the owned list: discovery is moving to AI answers that send little traffic.
- **Marketplace and platform.** Two audiences with different strategies: buyers get matches and inspiration, sellers get performance summaries with one actionable suggestion. Transactional email must be near-instant. Track retention on both sides.

## Regulated verticals

Healthcare, financial services, gambling and wagering, and anything touching prescription medicines carry sector rules on top of email law (health privacy, financial promotions, mandatory gambling messages, medicine advertising). This file sets no gates for them: read the sector regulator's current guidance at the primary source before drafting, and keep legal review in the loop.
